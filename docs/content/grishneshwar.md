# Grishneshwar (Ghrishneshwar, Ghushmeshwar), Verul, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar district, Maharashtra: research notes

Story data: `src/temples/stories/grishneshwar.json`. Researched 2026-10-07.

**No official trust website was found.** The temple is run by the Shri Grishneshwar (Ghrushneshwar) Mandir Devasthan Trust, Verul; its president is Kunal Dandge (Pudhari, 1 Oct 2026). grishneshwarjyotirling.org is "powered by YatraDham.Org", and grishneshwartemple.com says it is "a private facilitator and not affiliated with the Grishneshwar Temple Trust". Both were used only for practical details (rules, timings), which are flagged as secondary.

## 1. Legend: Shiva Purana, Kotirudra Samhita, ch. 32–33
- ch. 32 (https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226533.html): Sudharma, a devout Brahmin, and his wife Sudeha live near a mountain called Deva in the southern region. Childless, Sudeha urges him to marry her sister Ghushma (Ghuśmā). Ghushma makes "hundred and one earthen idols every day" and in time "completed a hundred-thousand images"; she bears a son, and Sudeha becomes jealous ("the fire in my heart can be tranquilised only through the tears of Ghuśmā", ch. 33 v.12–17).
- ch. 33 (https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226534.html):
  - vv. 12–17: Sudeha kills the son at night, cuts the body to pieces and throws them into the lake (where Ghushma immersed her lingas).
  - vv. 24–29: Ghushma continues her worship without grief: "He who has given this boy will protect him too."
  - vv. 30–31: when she casts the earthen lingas into the lake, she sees her son alive on the bank.
  - vv. 34–35: Shiva appears and offers to kill Sudeha with his trident.
  - vv. 38–40: Ghushma pleads for her sister.
  - vv. 42–48: at her request Shiva stays as the linga Ghuśmeśa, and blesses her family for 101 generations.
  - vv. 45–46: the lake is called **Śivālaya**.
  - Translator's note: "near Devagiri in Aurangabad district".
- Short Smaranam: "…Himalaye tu Kedaram, **Ghushmesham cha Shivalaye**".
- Not used: the variant that Parvati, rubbing vermilion in her palm, produced the linga (the "ghrishna" etymology).

## 2. History

| Date | Event | Source |
|------|-------|--------|
| 8th c. | Kailasa temple (Ellora Cave 16) carved under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I; the place was known as Elapura | general / Ellora sources |
| 13th–14th c. | "The temple structure was destroyed by the Delhi Sultanate in 13th and 14th centuries", then repeatedly rebuilt and destroyed | Wikipedia, Grishneshwar Temple. The narration says "damaged in the wars of the thirteenth and fourteenth centuries" (non-communal phrasing). |
| 16th c. | Maloji Bhosale (c. 1552–1605), grandfather of Shivaji, "carried out the restoration of the Grishneshwar temple near Verul, and also constructed a large tank at the Shambhu Mahadev temple in Shikhar Shingnapur"; the treasure story is a tradition (Wikipedia notes historians dispute the treasure account) | Wikipedia, Maloji Bhosale; marathwadatourism.com |
| — | Verul is the ancestral village of the Bhosales; the samadhis of Babaji, Maloji and Vithoji Bhosale stand in front of the temple | durgbharari.in (a secondary heritage blog) |
| 18th c. | Rebuilt in the present form under **Ahilyabai Holkar** (marathwadatourism.com, most guides). **Conflict:** Wikipedia now says "rebuilt in 1729 under the sponsorship of queen Gautama Bai Holkar". The narration says "most accounts credit her [Ahilyabai] with raising this one in its present form". | |

- Architecture: red basalt (Wikipedia now says "black stone"; photos show red stone); court about 240 × 185 ft; five-tier shikhara; Dashavatara and Shiva-lila carvings; 24-pillar sabha mandapa; garbhagriha about 17 × 17 ft, below the mandapa level; the linga faces east (site model; matches photos).
- Ellora: about 1.5 km away (Wikipedia); UNESCO World Heritage Site 1983; about 34 caves, Buddhist, Hindu and Jain, roughly 6th–10th c.

## 3. Rituals (secondary sources)
- **Men enter the sanctum bare-chested** (Wikipedia: "men must go bare-chested"; grishneshwartemple.com).
- **Devotees may touch the linga** (grishneshwarjyotirling.org: "allowed to touch the Shivling during darshan, but it may depend on … crowd conditions"; grishneshwartemple.com).
- Aarti: morning about 5:00, bhog about 12:00, evening about 8:00 (grishneshwarjyotirling.org). Darshan about 5:30 AM – 9:00 PM. Open all night on Mahashivratri.
- **News**: Pudhari (1 Oct 2026) reports that darshan will be closed for about six months from 1 Nov 2026 for underground-passage works, with three daily pujas continuing inside. eSakal (2 Oct 2026) reports the collector's assurance that it will not stay closed for six months. Not narrated; noted in `aarti.notes`.

## 4. Stotram
- Long Dvadasha Jyotirlinga Stotram, v.12 (sanskritdocuments.org): "इलापुरे रम्यविशालकेऽस्मिन् समुल्लसन्तं च जगद्वरेण्यम्। वन्दे महोदारतरस्वभावं घृष्णेश्वराख्यं शरणं प्रपद्ये॥". It is the twelfth in both the long Stotram and the short Smaranam.
- Translation (mine): "Here in Ilapura, lovely and wide, he shines forth, most worthy of the whole world's worship. I bow to him, whose nature is boundlessly generous; to him called Ghrishneshwara I go for refuge."

## 5. Aarti

Temple 360 offers a live camera, but it is not YouTube and not the trust. grishneshwartemple.com lists a YouTube handle @GRISHNESHWAR_TEMPLE, but that site disclaims any trust affiliation, so it is not used. `aarti.youtube` is left empty; the synthesized aarti plays.
- Lyrics in the JSON: "**Lavathavati vikrala**", the Marathi Shankar aarti by Samarth Ramdas (1608–1681), public domain. The text was taken from tring.co.in/aarti/lavthavti-vikrala; printings differ in small spellings. It is the standard Shiva aarti of Maharashtra, but it is not documented as Grishneshwar's own text.
