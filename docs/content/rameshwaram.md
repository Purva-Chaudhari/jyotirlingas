# Rameshwaram (Ramanathaswamy Temple, Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu): research notes

Story data: `src/temples/stories/rameshwaram.json`. Researched 2026-10-07.

The official site is the TN HR&CE temple page, rameswaramramanathar.hrce.tn.gov.in (temple ID TM035671). Its home page gives the sthala purana in brief and the timings. Its Video Gallery and "Temple Functions LIVE" pages were empty when checked.

## 1. Legends

### Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 6.123.19–21 (valmikiramayan.net numbering)
https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/yuddha/sarga123/yuddhasans123.htm
- v.19: "atra pūrvaṃ mahādevaḥ prasādam akarot prabhuḥ": "At this place the lord Shiva formerly bestowed his grace on me" (said from the Pushpaka on the way home).
- vv.20–21: "Setubandha iti khyātam trailokyena ca pūjitam … etat pavitraṃ paramaṃ mahāpātaka nāśanam": a holy ford, adored in the three worlds, that destroys great sins.
- Other editions number this sarga 126.

### Shiva Purana, Kotirudra Samhita, ch. 31 ("The Greatness of Rameshvara")
https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226532.html
- Before the war, on the seashore, Rama makes an earthen (parthiva) linga and worships it before drinking water. Shiva appears, grants victory, and at Rama's request stays as **Rameshvara**.
- **Variant used alongside**: the temple's (HR&CE) version is set *after* the war. Rama worships "to expiate the sin of Brahmahatya"; Hanuman is sent to Kailash; Sita makes a sand linga; Hanuman's linga is installed separately and is worshipped first. The narration gives the temple version and then names the Purana's difference.

### Temple sthala purana (HR&CE; tntemplesproject; shivatemples.com)
- Rama "consecrated and worshipped the Sivalinga to expiate the sin of Brahmahatya" (HR&CE). Ravana was the son of Vishrava and grandson of Pulastya.
- Hanuman is delayed, Sita shapes the sand linga, Rama installs it. Hanuman is "disheartened" and his linga is installed beside it. "Even today, this Lingam (the one installed by Hanuman) is prayed to first" (tntemplesproject).
- **Hanuman's tail**: the story that Hanuman tried to pull the sand linga out with his tail is a widely told popular version. No official source for it was found, so the narration says "a well-loved telling says".
- **Agni Theertham name**: according to the TN Tourism page and popular accounts, Agni bathed here after Sita's fire ordeal to wash off the sin of having touched her. The narration presents this as "local tradition" and leaves out the "sin of touching" detail.
- The 22 theerthams stand for Rama's 22 arrows (Wikipedia). Pilgrims start at Agni Theertham and finish at Kodi Theertham (shivatemples.com).

Figures: the Rajagopuram is 53 m tall; the third corridor has about 1,212 pillars, and all its sides total about 3,850 ft (Wikipedia; other sites say "over 1,200 m"); the compound is about 865 × 657 ft (Wikipedia); the temple covers about 15 acres (HR&CE). The Nandi is about 22 ft long and 17 ft high (site layout notes, from published figures).

## 3. Rituals and timings

- HR&CE (official): open 4:00 AM–1:00 PM and 3:00–8:00 PM; **Spatika Linga darshan 5:00–6:00 AM**; Moolasthana darshan 6:00 AM–1:00 PM; theertham bathing 5:30 AM–12:30 PM and 3:00–7:00 PM.
- Puja list (secondary: yatradham, myoksha): Palliyarai Deepa Aradhana 5:00 AM, Spadigalinga Deepa Aradhana 5:10, Thiruvananthal 5:45, Vila 7:00, Kalasandhi 10:00, Uchikala 12:00, Sayaratchai 6:00 PM, Arthajama 8:30, Palliyarai 8:45 PM.
- Worship order: the Vishwalingam (Kasi Viswanathar) first, then Ramanathaswamy.
- **Kashi–Rameshwaram yatra** (Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham, 2024 news; shivatemples.com): sand from Dhanushkodi or the Rameswaram shore (Kanchi: "Sikata taken at Dhanushkodi", shaped into a Saikata linga) is immersed in the Ganga. Kanchi did this at Dashashwamedh Ghat, and some accounts say Prayag. Ganga water is then brought back for the abhishekam of Ramanathaswamy, and "the Kasi Yatra is completed only after worshipping Ramanathaswamy" (temple channel's description). The narration puts the sand at Agni Theertham, as the brief asks, and names Dhanushkodi in the "did you know".

## 4. Aarti
- **Text**: the temple publishes no text. The JSON uses the public-domain Stotram verse.
- **Stotram verse** (sanskritdocuments.org): "सुताम्रपर्णीजलराशियोगे निबध्य सेतुं विशिखैरसङ्ख्यैः। श्रीरामचन्द्रेण समर्पितं तं रामेश्वराख्यं नियतं नमामि॥". This is **verse 9** of the full stotra. Rameshwaram is **7th** in the short "Saurashtre Somanatham" list, the order used by `catalog.ts`, so the narration says "seventh Jyotirlinga of the Stotram". Variant reading: "श्रीताम्रपर्णीं … निशि बिल्वपत्रैः … समर्चितं तं … सततं नमामि".
- **YouTube**: none is used. The HR&CE temple page links no video, and the department channel @TNHRCEdepartment (UC-2EruIuSwUW6-eqK88e7dQ, linked from hrce.tn.gov.in) has no Rameswaram aarti. The channel @ramanathaswamytemple (UC9CgVhkj8XZ2g__MWbVWr2g, joined 15 Jul 2020) streams the temple's hundiyal counting (most recently 01.09.2026), Aadi Thirukalyanam and kodiyetram. ## 5. Uncertain facts
- Muthuramalinga Sethupathi's lifespan ("did not live to be fifty"): from a secondary source ("lived for forty-nine years").
- The Agni-bath legend and Hanuman's tail are popular traditions, and the narration hedges both.
- The origin of the Spatika linga worship is often credited to Adi Shankara. That claim is not used. The 2021 Sringeri gift is documented.
- The individual puja times come from secondary sources.
