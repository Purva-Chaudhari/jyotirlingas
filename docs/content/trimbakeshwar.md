## 1. The legend: Shiva Purana, Kotirudra Samhita, ch. 24–27 (Shastri translation)

- ch. 24, https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226525.html
  - Gautama and "his wife Ahalyā [who] was extremely virtuous", on Brahmagiri.
  - "A terrible drought lasting for a hundred years".
  - Six months of penance to Varuna, who says, "How can I countermand the behest of the gods?"
  - A ditch "of a hand's depth", filled with perennial water.
  - v.19: "Charitable gifts made here… the Śrāddha offered to the manes, everything will be imperishable" (used in stop 5).
  - Thousands of sages settle there.
- ch. 25, …/doc226526.html
  - The wives turn Gautama's disciples away from the water; Ahalya fetches it herself; the wives slander her.
  - The sages propitiate Ganesha, who warns them: "Those who become furious without any offence are sure to incur loss… the sage Gautama afforded you happiness".
  - "Gaṇeśa assumed the form of a feeble cow"; Gautama "warded off the cow with a bunch of grass blades", and it falls dead.
  - Stoning and expulsion.
  - The atonement: circle the earth or Brahmagiri, bring the Ganga, and worship "a crore of his phallic images" (earthen lingas). Ahalya joins him.
- ch. 26, …/doc226527.html
  - Shiva: "You are already sinless. You have been deceived."
  - Gautama: "If they had not done like this, how could I have had your vision?"
  - "May Gaṅgā be given to me. Render help to the worlds."
  - Ganga appears as a woman and wants to return; she asks Shiva to stay with his consort.
  - The gods: "When Jupiter… comes to the zodiac of Leo all of us will come."
  - "Gaṅgā there became famous as **Gautamī**. The phallic image became famous by the name **Tryambaka**."
- ch. 27, …/doc226528.html
  - Ganga descends from Brahmagiri "from the branches of an Udumbara tree" (a cluster fig, *gular*) at **Gangadvāra**.
  - She vanishes on seeing the hostile sages; Gautama pleads for them; they circumambulate the mountain.
  - "A ditch named **Kuśāvarta** just beneath Gaṅgādvāra was made by Gautama".
  - **Not used**: the "other kalpa" variant (vv.23–46), in which Gautama curses the brahmins to become non-Shaivas and settle in Kanchi. It is sectarian, so the forgiving version is told.
- "Kusha-avarta", Ganga turned back by a ring of kusha grass, is popular etymology; it is narrated as "many say".

- Wikipedia says the temple was "destroyed by Aurangzeb" before the rebuild. This is not used (communal framing; the trust says only "the site of an old temple").
- **Sant Nivruttinath**, elder brother and guru of Sant Dnyaneshwar, took samadhi at Trimbakeshwar; "Lakhs of Varkaris" gather on his punyatithi (trust). Used in the stop 1 did-you-know.
- The trust's /about page also lists the old establishment, including "Two devotees for circumambulation of Bramhagiri Mountain" (used in the stop 3 did-you-know) and a jewel-studded "thorn" (crest) brought back by Bhausaheb Peshwa (not used).

## 3. Rituals and living culture

- **The linga**: "in the form of a three faced embodying Tridev, Lord Bramha, Lord Vishnu and Lord Shiva" (trust about). There are three small lingas in a hollow, with water welling up (Wikipedia; the site model).
- **Daily program** (trust): Morning Puja, "Puja of Bramha Deva", 7:00–8:30 AM; Mid-Day Puja, "Puja of Mahadev", 10:45 AM–12:30 PM; Evening Puja, "Vishnu Puja" (the page repeats "7.00 AM TO 8.30 AM", evidently a typo). Darshan is about 5:30 AM–9 PM (travel sites).
- **Mukut and mask**: the trust gallery has "सुवर्ण पंचमुखी मुखवटा व पांडवकालीन हिरे-रत्नजडीत किरीट" (a golden five-faced mask and a Pandava-era diamond- and jewel-studded crown). On Kartik Padva "Golden mask is placed on the Shiva ling". Mondays and festivals are from travel sites and the site model; narrated as tradition.
- **Monday palkhi**: "On every Monday palanquin procession of Trimbakeshwar is taken to Kushavarta Tirtha and pooja performed there" (trust festivals). Travel sites say 4–5 PM.
- **Simhastha Kumbh**: "The Shashisnan (most sacred bath) in Sinhastha is performed at Kushavarta" (trust about). The 2026–28 Simhastha opens with dhwajarohan on **31 Oct 2026**; Trimbakeshwar's amrit snans are on 2 Aug, 31 Aug and 12 Sep 2027 (The Week / PTI, 1 Jun 2025). The narration says "the next Simhastha opens at the end of October 2026", which will date; update after 2028.
- **Narayan Nagbali**: a three-day rite for those who died untimely deaths (Narayan bali) and for the sin of killing a cobra (Nag bali), traditionally (citing Dharma Sindhu) performed only at Trimbakeshwar by tamrapatradhari tirtha purohits. **Kalsarp shanti** and **Tripindi shraddha** are also performed (Wikipedia "Narayan nagbali"; purohit sites). Narrated as "tradition holds".

## 4. Stotram

Verse 10: "Sahyādriśīrṣe vimale vasantaṁ godāvarītīrapavitradeśe | yaddarśanāt pātakam āśu nāśaṁ prayāti taṁ tryambakam īśam īḍe ||". Translated in the story as: "He who dwells on the pure heights of the Sahyadri, in the holy land on the Godavari's bank, whose darshan swiftly destroys all sin: I praise that Lord, Tryambaka." The short verse has "Tryambakaṁ Gautamītaṭe" (10th).

## 6. Uncertain facts

- The Nassak diamond's link to Nanasaheb is unverified, and narrated as "a story, which historians say cannot be confirmed".
- The days the mukut is shown (Mondays and festivals) and the palkhi time come from travel sources.
- The evening puja time is a typo on the trust page.
- The reign end year differs by source: 1760 (trust) or 1761 (Wikipedia).
