The walk goes clockwise round the temple (keeping it on the right, as in pradakshina): the west side, Bhim Shila behind the shrine, east to the gateway in the protection wall and down into the Samadhi court, then down the east side to the front, Nandi, the hall and the sanctum. The approach street (zone `path`, behind the spawn) has no stop; stop 1 points back at it.

## 1. Legends

### Nara–Narayana: Shiva Purana, Kotirudra Samhita, ch. 19
https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226520.html
- vv. 1–4: Nara and Narayana, incarnations of Vishnu, do penance at Badarikashrama, worshipping an earthen (parthiva) linga.
- vv. 5–11: Shiva, pleased, offers a boon; they ask him to stay; he stays at Kedara as Kedareshvara.
- vv. 13–17: when the Pandavas come, Shiva "assumed the form of a **buffalo**" and fled; they caught him (the translation says by the tail); the **head** portion became fixed at **Nayapala** (Nepal) and the body remained at Kedara.

### The Pandavas and the Panch Kedar
- **BKTC** (About Kedarnath): after the war the Pandavas sought Shiva's blessing to atone; he "took refuge at Kedarnath in form of a buffalo" and dived into the ground, leaving the hump, the conical rock worshipped as Sadashiva.
- **Folk / Garhwal tradition** (Wikipedia, Kedarnath Temple): Shiva hid at Guptakashi; then as a **bull** among cattle; Bhima straddled two hills (or mountains) and caught it as it sank; hump at Kedarnath, arms at Tungnath, face at Rudranath, navel/belly at Madhyamaheshwar, hair at Kalpeshwar (the Panch Kedar).
- Narration: "in the form of a buffalo; many in these hills tell it as a bull", and Guptakashi, Bhima and the two hills under "tradition says".

### Ghee on the rock
Wikipedia (Kedarnath Temple) says rituals include massaging the linga with ghee, linked by tradition to the Pandavas/Bhima. The narration says "a practice tradition links to the Pandavas themselves" and nothing stronger.

- Altitude: Wikipedia 3,583 m; BKTC says 3,581 m and calls it the highest of the Jyotirlingas. Trek: BKTC "16 km" from Gaurikund; Wikipedia "17 km".
- **The Rawal** (chief priest) traditionally comes from the Veerashaiva (Lingayat) community of Karnataka. The narration names only "the Rawal", because sources conflict on which Rawal reopened the temple in 2013 (one news report names Bhimashankar Ling Shivacharya; Wikipedia names Vageesha Lingacharya as the Rawal in 2013).
- Adi Shankaracharya: birth in Kerala, the age of 32 and the samadhi at Kedarnath are all given as "tradition says". Other traditions place his end elsewhere (e.g. Kanchi).
- Mandapa images (Wikipedia): the five Pandavas, Krishna, Nandi, Virabhadra, Parvati, Draupadi.

## 3. Rituals and living culture

**BKTC**
- Morning abhisheks from 4:00 AM; bookable pujas: Mahabhishek (₹8,500), Rudrabhishek (₹6,500), Shodashopachar (₹5,000), Evening Aarti (₹2,500), evening paths, and Bhog in several forms.
- "Evening Aarti will start around 06:00 PM (During Summer season) till 08:30 PM."
- Closes on the first day of Kartik (in practice Bhai Dooj) and reopens in Vaishakh; the winter seat is Ukhimath.
- Morning: pilgrims touch the rock and apply ghee (this is widely reported, but restrictions on crowded days are possible; the narration says "in the early hours, pilgrims come close…"). Evening: shringar and aarti.
- At Ukhimath, the winter worship of Kedarnath is shared with Madhyamaheshwar (Wikipedia, Kedarnath).

## 4. Stotram
- Long Dvadasha Jyotirlinga Stotram (attributed to Adi Shankaracharya), Kedara verse, from sanskritdocuments.org (dvAdashaJyotistotra): "महाद्रिपार्श्वे च तटे रमन्तं सम्पूज्यमानं सततं मुनीन्द्रैः। सुरासुरैर्यक्षमहोरगाढ्यैः केदारमीशं शिवमेकमीडे॥". **Note:** in that text it is the **7th** verse (its verses follow a different order). Kedarnath is the **11th** Jyotirlinga in the short Smaranam ("…Himalaye tu Kedaram, Ghushmesham cha Shivalaye"), which `catalog.ts` follows, and BKTC also calls it the eleventh. The narration therefore says "the eleventh Jyotirlinga of the Stotram" and does not give a verse number.
- Translation (mine): "He who delights on the flank of the great mountain, on its high slopes (tate), worshipped always by the greatest sages, by gods and demons, yakshas and great serpents: I praise that One, the Lord Kedara, Shiva."

## 5. Aarti
- **YouTube: none.** BKTC's official channel (@bktcofficial; 5 videos and about 35 shorts, listed with yt-dlp on 7 Oct 2026) has no aarti recording and no live stream. Its videos are news (CM and Governor visits), door closings, and two Ukhimath winter-yatra clips. I checked both (El2WdQ51ems is an interview; 6a4dE6k4UC0 is speeches and a bhajan). The second channel "Shri Badarinath kedarnath Temple Committee" (UCEQuF2Whhhlyo8YCgXQSYeg) has two old videos (a logo and a 2022 notice). badrinath-kedarnath.gov.in/aboutus/videos.aspx embeds only El2WdQ51ems, 4C7iWPekYRE, LZutxpzezN8 and NmWRcWETYrY, none of them an aarti. So `aarti.youtube` is left empty; the synthesized aarti plays. Revisit during the yatra season (May–Oct), when BKTC may stream.
- Lyrics in the JSON: "Karpuragauram", the traditional verse sung at the waving of lamps; public domain. It is not documented as Kedarnath's exact aarti text. The popular "Jai Kedar Udar Shankar" has an unknown author and date, so it is not used.
